# Minerals: purity, grade, refining and the cut-over (2026-09-30)

Status: decision record from a spoken session. Continues
`2026-09-30-generated-minerals.md` and folds in `sim-game/design/properties.md`
and `sim-game/design/starting-zone.md`, which remain discussion documents,
not authorities. Where this note and those documents disagree, this note
wins; where this note is silent, they are still open.

## Summary

Every world generates its own mineral species, each with a fixed six-number
property sheet, and purity is rounded into letter grades that scale some of
those numbers hard enough that a well-refined weak mineral can beat a badly
refined strong one. A tiered refining process raises grade, the first smelter
can be built from raw ore of any mineral and is capped by that mineral's heat
tolerance, and world generation must prove a climbable starter ladder around
spawn. The existing named ores, recipes, saves and golden hash are replaced in
one cut-over, and the old rule that purity rises with distance from spawn is
withdrawn until purity's source is decided.

## Decisions

1. **Purity affects both yield and product.** Mining a deposit of purity P
   yields more usable material than one of lower purity, and parts made from
   it inherit its purity via grade. Test: same mineral, higher purity in,
   higher grade part out and more ore per unit mined.
2. **Purity is rounded into letter grades (A, B, C to start).** Items of the
   same species and grade stack and are interchangeable in recipes; items of
   different grades never share a stack. Test: two stacks of one species at
   purities within the same band merge; across bands they do not.
3. **Grade is a real multiplier, not a bonus.** A C-grade part from a hard
   mineral can lose to an A-grade part from a softer one. Test: there exist
   two species X (harder) and Y (softer) generated by the sim such that
   effective hardness of Y at grade A exceeds X at grade C.
4. **Grade scales only where impurity plausibly weakens the material.**
   Strength, hardness, reactivity and conductivity scale with grade; density
   and heat tolerance are fixed per species. Test: for one species, density
   and heat tolerance read identically at grades A and C; the other four do
   not.
5. **The property sheet is six whole numbers per species:** density,
   strength, hardness, heat tolerance, reactivity, conductivity. Conductivity
   is generated and shown from day one but no rule reads it until power
   exists. Test: the inspector shows all six for every species; grep for
   conductivity in `step.rs` finds no rule.
6. **A tiered refining process exists and raises grade.** Rung one is
   mechanical: time plus mass loss (crush and separate, most of the pile
   discarded). Rung two costs heat and fuel. Rung three waits on later tech.
   Exact costs and machines are not decided. Test: a sim command chain turns
   C-grade ore into B-grade material with fewer units out than in.
7. **Purity rising with distance from spawn is withdrawn.** The sentences
   stating it in `CLAUDE.md` and `GAME.md` are struck, and the distance term
   is removed from `worldgen` so purity is a seeded per-deposit roll with no
   spatial trend. This changes the golden determinism hash; the commit must
   say so. Test: correlation between chunk distance and deposit purity in the
   test world is not significantly positive; the world test that asserts the
   trend is removed or inverted.
8. **First contact shows a rough partial sheet that sharpens with better
   tools.** Side note to explore later: readings may be expressed relative to
   the player's current capability ("harder than your drill") rather than as
   absolute numbers. Test: a fresh deposit shows ranges or blanks; after an
   assay action or better tool the same deposit shows exact values.
9. **A smelter can be built from raw ore of any species.** Its maximum
   running temperature is capped by its build material's heat tolerance, so a
   furnace made of low-tolerance rock processes only low-tolerance ore.
   Test: build two smelters from species with different heat tolerance; the
   lower one refuses ore the higher one accepts.
10. **World generation guarantees a climbable starter ladder around spawn**
    via a reachability check: a hand-gatherable rung zero that includes a
    fuel lightable by hand, then successive rungs each gated by a new
    requirement. Test: for one hundred seeds, the check reports at least N
    rungs reachable with N from tuning.
11. **Rung zero is abundant, not renewable.** A player who wastes all of it
    can soft-lock the world; that is accepted. Test: rung-zero deposits are
    finite, and their total exceeds the ladder's needs by the margin in
    tuning.
12. **Named ores are cut over in one go.** `OreKind`, the four ore items,
    named recipes, save version 8 and the golden hash are replaced by
    species-and-grade equivalents in a single change with a save-version and
    protocol-version bump; no compatibility shim. Test: no `Iron`, `Copper`,
    `Coal` or `Stone` identifier remains in `sim`; `first_plate.rs` passes
    against a generated species.
13. **Species get a generated name at discovery; the discoverer may rename.**
    The discoverer can rename later and can grant rename rights to other
    players. Renames are player commands through the tick so peers agree.
    Test: rename by a non-discoverer without a grant is refused; after a
    grant it succeeds and all peers hash identically.

## Open questions

- Where does purity come from now that distance is withdrawn? Candidates: a
  flat seeded roll, depth, region, or something tied to the starter ladder.
- Exact grade bands: three letters (A, B, C) to start, but the purity
  thresholds and whether more letters appear later are unset.
- Refining specifics: what building or hand action does rung one, what the
  mass-loss ratio is, and what "later tech" means for rung three.
- Whether refined material of one species can ever exceed the grade of the
  best deposit of that species found so far.
- How partial sheets sharpen: which tool or action reveals which number, and
  whether readings are relative to the player's gear.
- How many species per world, and the deposit-density rule for the starting
  zone (today's one-per-chunk is probably too sparse for the ladder).
- Generated look and generated name style for species; the art brief wants
  shape and colour to read at 1×.
- Whether the "ore is weaker than refined" step factor in `properties.md`
  survives contact with grade, since grade already gives a climb.
- Co-op: whether the discoverer's rename permission list is per species or a
  general trust setting.

## Suggested next steps

1. Strike the distance-purity wording from `CLAUDE.md` and `GAME.md` (done
   in this session) and remove the distance term from `worldgen.rs`; update
   the world test and golden hash, and say so in the commit.
2. Add `docs/adr/` with the convention README, template and ADR 0001 for
   this note (done in this session); future decisions get their own ADR.
3. Write a one-page spec for `MineralSpecies { id, generated_name,
   player_name, discoverer, rename_grants, sheet }` in `sim`, generated from
   seed, with deposits referencing a species id and a purity.
4. Define `Grade` (A, B, C) with purity thresholds in `tuning.rs`, and the
   grade-scaling rule for the four scaled properties.
5. Rewrite the existing recipes (plate, gear, smelter) as property thresholds
   and make the smelter buildable from raw ore with a heat-tolerance cap.
6. Plan and execute the one-go cut-over: items keyed by species and grade,
   recipes by property, save version 9, protocol bump, new golden hash,
   `first_plate.rs` against a generated species.
7. Implement the starter-ladder reachability check in worldgen with a
   hundred-seed test.
8. Add the first-contact partial sheet and an assay action, headless first,
   with an inspector panel.
