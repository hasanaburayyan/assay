# Commercial Viability of MOBAs and MMOs for Small (2-5 Engineer) Indie Studios

Research date: 2026-09-29. Figures are as reported by the cited source at the time of publication; CCU = concurrent users. Items marked **[EST]** are third-party estimates (SteamDB-derived revenue calculators, Sensor Tower, and similar tools) and should be treated as rough. Items marked **[LOW-QUALITY SOURCE]** come from aggregators, forums, or market-report mills and need careful handling.

---

## 1. MOBA market: concentration, shutdowns (2014-2026), and indie/adjacent successes

### Takeaway
The MOBA market is a winner-take-most oligopoly held by a handful of 10+ year incumbents (Honor of Kings, League of Legends, Dota 2, Mobile Legends). Well-funded challengers of all sizes keep failing: EA, Epic, Blizzard, NCSoft, Netmarble, Hi-Rez, a $19M-Series-A studio (Odyssey) and a heavily funded ex-Riot studio (Theorycraft) all shut down, sunset, or heavily scaled back their games. The only newcomer with real traction in 2024-2026 is Valve's Deadlock, and it has Valve's distribution behind it. I found no documented case of a 2-5 person team shipping a commercially successful traditional MOBA in this period.

### Inferences
- Every well-capitalized Western MOBA launched after about 2014 has failed or been reduced to maintenance, with Deadlock as the one exception. Deadlock benefits from Valve's platform, brand, and zero-cost audience access. If $19M-funded teams of 40 and AAA publishers cannot break through, a 2-5 engineer team building a direct LoL/Dota/Smite competitor has close to no base rate of success.
- The pattern is consistent. Strong launch or playtest CCU (Supervive 48k, Throne-and-Liberty-style spikes) decays 90%+ within months. Once the population falls below the matchmaking threshold, the game dies faster.
- Even the "MOBA-lite" reframes (Omega Strikers, Battlerite, Supervive) failed. Changing the format does not remove the liquidity problem if the game still needs live 3v3 or 5v5 matchmaking at scale.

- No confirmed indie (2-5 person) traditional-MOBA success was found. Bloons TD Battles (Ninja Kiwi) and similar tower-defense-PvP successes were not researched in depth. Ninja Kiwi is not a 2-5 person studio.

## 2. Key MOBA barriers for small teams

### Takeaway
The main barrier is player liquidity. A competitive team game needs thousands of concurrent players per region and skill band to make fast, fair matches. Once CCU drops, queue times and match quality spiral down, which drives more players away. Balance and hero-content cadence, anti-cheat, esports expectations, and F2P monetization economics add ongoing operating costs that sank studios with 40 to several hundred employees.

### Cited Findings
- Retention, not launch size, is the failure point. Stunlock called retention "the biggest challenge" and saw "diminishing returns on gameplay changes and marketing campaigns". — [Stunlock Blog](https://blog.stunlock.com/the-future-of-battlerite/)
- Odyssey said Omega Strikers suffered "high player turnover and an inability to grow", and that as a 40-person studio it "needed to ensure they could keep the lights on". — [TechRaptor](https://techraptor.net/gaming/news/omega-strikers-will-cease-content-development-8-months-after-release-as-studio-moves-on)
- Supervive fell from about 48k peak to about 100 active players and was shut down for "dwindling player numbers". — [Out of Games](https://outof.games/news/8620-supervive-is-shutting-down-in-february-2026-due-to-a-lack-of-attention/); [KitGuru](https://www.kitguru.net/gaming/joao-silva/theorycraft-games-shutting-down-supervive-just-five-months-after-1-0-launch/)
- Adding modes splits the population. Battlerite's community blamed the Battlerite Royale spin-off for splitting players. This is community opinion, not a studio statement. — [Stunlock Blog / Steam discussions summary](https://blog.stunlock.com/the-future-of-battlerite/)
- Server reliability can kill a relaunch. Gigantic: Rampage Edition's servers "crumbled" at launch and were never fully fixed. Battlerite's F2P launch broke its CCU record and then its host went down. — [Massively OP](https://massivelyop.com/2024/04/10/gigantics-buy-to-play-relaunch-is-being-tripped-up-by-ongoing-server-connection-issues/); [Stunlock Blog](https://blog.stunlock.com/rewards_and_servers/)
- Content cadence: Deadlock's population "rises and falls in a predictable pattern where big updates drive player surges followed by decay", even for Valve. The update treadmill drives population. — [Deadlock player-count aggregator (therockpit)](https://therockpit.net/wp-content/pages/deadlock-player-count.html) **[LOW-QUALITY SOURCE]**
- Even incumbents cut costs. Hi-Rez's layoffs hit its design, production, and esports teams, which shows that esports and content upkeep are among the first costs cut when revenue falls. — [MMOs.com](https://mmos.com/news/hi-rez-studios-conducts-second-round-of-layoffs-in-four-months-amid-smite-2-push)

### Inferences
- **Liquidity math (inference, not sourced):** a 5v5 game with ranked tiers and 3+ regions needs 10 players per match times the number of skill bands times the number of regions, plus acceptable queue times. In practice that means at least low thousands of CCU per region at off-peak hours. The collapses above (Smite 2 at about 2k CCU in 2024, Predecessor at about 2k, Supervive at about 100) show that 1-2k CCU is fragile even with a large team, and a few hundred CCU means near-certain death.
- Network effects work against newcomers. Players' friends, sunk hero and skin collections, and ranked identity are all tied to incumbents. That is why even well-reviewed games (Battlerite, Supervive) couldn't hold retention.
- Balance work grows roughly with heroes × items × maps. A 2-5 engineer team would spend most of its time on balance, anti-cheat, and live-ops instead of new content.
- Mitigations a small team could use: bots or backfill AI, smaller match sizes (1v1, 2v2), async play, co-op PvE modes that don't need liquidity, and region-merged servers.

### Gaps
- No published matchmaking population thresholds (for example a GDC talk quantifying "minimum CCU per region") were found in this research pass.
- No sourced data on anti-cheat costs for small studios.
- No sourced figures for esports budgets.

## 3. MMO market concentration (2014-2026)

### Takeaway
The MMO market is dominated by long-running incumbents (WoW, FFXIV, OSRS/RuneScape, ESO, Tibia, Albion, BDO). Big-budget newcomers have a poor 2024-2026 record. Amazon's New World is shutting down (servers off 2027-01-31). Throne and Liberty lost about 95% of its Steam CCU. Ashes of Creation's studio collapsed about 7 weeks after early access. Pax Dei shrank its team before 1.0. Old, low-fidelity games (OSRS, Tibia) are among the healthiest, which suggests nostalgia, low hardware requirements, and a stable ruleset can beat production value.

### Cited Findings
- **OSRS** set an all-time record of about 240,756 CCU on 2025-08-03. RuneScape (RS3 + OSRS) passed $3B lifetime revenue. — [TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/old-schoolrunescape-concurrent-player-count-high-record-240000-august-2025/); [GameSpot](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/old-school-runescape-is-more-popular-than-ever-breaks-concurrent-player-record/1100-6533697/); [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/runescape-surpasses-3bn-in-lifetime-revenue-as-jagex-marks-25th-anniversary/)

### Inferences
- Concentration is extreme. The durable winners are 10-25 year-old games, and several are "low-fi" (OSRS, Tibia, Albion's isometric style). 2024-2026 big-budget entrants (New World, T&L, Ashes, Pax Dei) mostly failed or shrank. Money and team size did not protect them.
- The healthy niche is games with modest graphics and a strong, specific loop (skilling, sandbox economy, PvP) that monetize a loyal base for many years. Tibia runs with about 100 staff, not 5, so even the "small" successes are not 2-5 engineer operations at their current scale.

### Gaps
- No sourced 2025-2026 figures were gathered for WoW, FFXIV, ESO, Black Desert, or Lost Ark. None of these publishers disclose MMO-specific subscriber or revenue numbers regularly. The writer should not invent them.
- No sourced Star Citizen crowdfunding total was gathered in this pass. It is widely reported as above $800M, but I did not verify that here.

## 4. Small-team and indie MMO case studies

### Takeaway
Indie MMOs can survive, but the successes are rare, slow (often 5-10+ years), and usually modest. The best-documented small-team outcomes are:
- **Legends of IdleOn**: a solo-dev "idle MMO" and a large commercial success.
- **Project Gorgon**: a husband-and-wife team that ran out of money in 2023 but shipped 1.0 in Jan 2026 after 8 years of early access.
- **Brighter Shores**: made by RuneScape's creator Andrew Gower. It launched to about 20k CCU and was down to about 250 within a year.
- **Dreadmyst**: a solo dev who drew 6k+ CCU and then abandoned the game within weeks.
- **Monsters & Memories**: run by a volunteer team.

Albion Online is the standout "started small" success, but it reached scale with a studio and later sold for about €100M+.

### Cited Findings
- **Legends of IdleOn** (LavaFlame2, solo dev, 2D idle MMO): described as a solo indie project. Its two main apps (IdleOn, Idle Skilling) each have 1M+ installs. — [IdleOn Wiki](https://idleon.wiki/wiki/IdleOn:About); [AppBrain](https://www.appbrain.com/dev/LavaFlame2/). **[EST / LOW-QUALITY SOURCE]** A review site claims about $1M/month gross and a single day of about $100k in early 2024 (top 30 on Steam). — [Fayiette](https://fayiette.com/review/idleon/). **[UNRELIABLE]** Raijin claims about $38.8M gross and about 4.0M units, but its "release date Nov 6, 2025" is wrong (the game launched on Steam years earlier), so I do not trust its figures. — [Raijin](https://raijin.gg/app/1476970/IdleOn/sales-revenue)
- **Project Gorgon** (Elder Game, led by husband and wife Eric Heimburg and Sandra Powers, both EverQuest and Asheron's Call veterans; about 3 people as of late 2023): in Nov 2023 Heimburg said revenue "dried up and so did their personal savings". With no full-time employees left, the team moved to part-time development to keep servers running. The game left Steam early access after 8 years (full release reported 2026-01-28). **[EST]** Revenue is estimated at about $2.2M lifetime on Steam. — [Massively OP](https://massivelyop.com/2023/11/11/were-out-of-money-project-gorgon-moves-to-part-time-development-to-keep-its-server-lights-on/); [MMORPG.com](https://www.mmorpg.com/news/project-gorgon-runs-out-of-cash-but-plans-to-continue-development-2000129448); [Yahoo/PC Gamer](https://tech.yahoo.com/gaming/articles/fantasy-mmo-husband-wife-duo-210227439.html); [games-stats.com](https://games-stats.com/steam/game/project-gorgon/); [Kickstarter](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/projectgorgon/project-gorgon-pc-mmo)
- **Brighter Shores** (Fen Research, Andrew Gower, co-creator of RuneScape; free-to-play with a premium option): early access on 2024-11-06 with a peak of about 17-20k CCU. Averages fell from about 8k in the first month to about 2.2k in Dec 2024, about 1k in Jan 2025, and about 250 (peak about 400) by Oct 2025, a 97% decline. Reviews were 69% positive overall (8,000+) and 55% recent. Cited reasons include no clear hook, shallow combat, professions lacking depth, confusing UI and progression, and an unfinished foundation. MMOBomb describes the game as in "maintenance mode". — [MMOBomb (Oct 3, 2025)](https://www.mmobomb.com/from-launch-to-life-support-brighter-shores-lost-97-of-players); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brighter_Shores); [SteamDB](https://steamdb.info/app/2791440/charts/)
- **Dreadmyst** (solo dev, custom C/C++/OpenGL engine, retro tab-target, fully free and "non-profit" with no monetization): launched on Steam on 2026-01-09 with 6k+ CCU. By late Feb 2026 the dev had halted work and pulled the game from Steam "again". It was still online for existing owners as of June 2026. A German outlet ran the headline that the launch came with a community warning about the developer. — [MMORPG.com](https://www.mmorpg.com/news/free-to-play-retro-indie-mmo-dreadmyst-is-burning-up-the-steam-charts-2000136998); [Massively OP](https://massivelyop.com/2026/02/27/dreadmysts-solo-dev-apparently-halted-work-on-the-mmorpg-and-yanked-it-off-steam-again/); [Mein-MMO](https://mein-mmo.de/en/new-mmorpg-on-steam-launches-today-but-warns-the-community-about-the-developer,1545206/)
- **Monsters & Memories** (Niche Worlds Cult, classic EverQuest-style): built by a volunteer team. It is preparing beta, early access, and "Module 1" at the same time, with early access targeted for 2026. — [Massively OP](https://massivelyop.com/2025/12/08/monsters-and-memories-is-prepping-for-beta-early-access-and-module-1-at-the-same-time/); [M&M Early Access FAQ](https://monstersandmemories.com/early-access-faq)
- **Embers Adrift** (Stormhaven Studios): still receiving updates in July 2026 ("content-light but optimized update"). It has a newer Steam listing (app 3336530). — [Massively OP (Jul 2026)](https://massivelyop.com/2026/07/29/indie-mmorpg-embers-adrift-has-a-content-light-but-optimized-update-for-players/); [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3336530/Embers_Adrift/)
- **Albion Online**: 389 MSEK revenue in 2025, sold to Stillfront for €100M+ (see section 3).
- **Tibia**: a small German team in 1997 that became a 100-person company with €24.5M turnover in 2023 (see section 3). This shows long-tail durability, but it is a 25+ year trajectory.

### Inferences
- The indie MMO pattern is a big launch spike followed by a 90%+ decline (Brighter Shores, Dreadmyst). A famous name (Gower) or a novel "free, no-monetization" pitch (Dreadmyst) does not solve retention or content depth.
- The small-team cases that last (Project Gorgon, Embers Adrift, M&M) do so by keeping costs very low: husband-and-wife teams, part-time devs, volunteers. Commercial returns are modest to negative.
- IdleOn is the clearest evidence that a solo dev can make serious money with an "MMO". It does this by removing the expensive parts: real-time combat netcode, a large shared world, and dense concurrency. Mechanically it is an idle and incremental game with light social and MMO features, sold on mobile and Steam.

## 5. MMO barriers: infrastructure, content treadmill, community, early access, and the crowdfunding trap

### Takeaway
For a small team, server cost is rarely the fatal expense at indie scale. The killers are (a) the content treadmill (players consume content far faster than 2-5 people can make it), (b) retention collapse after launch, (c) community and moderation load, and (d) funding runway, especially crowdfunding pre-sales that create obligations the team cannot meet. Large studios (Amazon, Intrepid, Mainframe) failed on the same axes, so a small team has less margin, not more.

### Cited Findings
- Running out of runway: Project Gorgon's revenue and personal savings both "dried up" in 2023, forcing part-time development. — [Massively OP](https://massivelyop.com/2023/11/11/were-out-of-money-project-gorgon-moves-to-part-time-development-to-keep-its-server-lights-on/)
- Crowdfunding trap, **Chronicles of Elyria**: raised $1.36M on Kickstarter in May 2016 (goal $900k) and nearly $8M total including store pre-sales. In 2020 Soulbound announced closure and laid off all staff, then reopened later that year. A backer class action filed in April 2020 was dismissed in 2022, and a separate arbitration against payment processor Xsolla proceeded. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chronicles_of_Elyria); [Massively OP](https://massivelyop.com/2022/10/12/chronicles-of-elyria-dev-celebrates-dismissal-of-backer-lawsuit-as-next-phase-of-heros-journey/); [MMOs.com](https://mmos.com/news/chronicles-of-elyria-canceled-studio-fires-all-staff); [MMORPG.com editorial](https://www.mmorpg.com/editorials/kickstarted-and-abandoned-the-chronicles-of-elyria-recipe-for-disaster-opinion-2000134261)
- Early access with governance and funding risk: Ashes of Creation (long crowdfunded and pre-sold) collapsed about 7 weeks after early access amid a board dispute, and was pulled from Steam. — [Game Informer](https://gameinformer.com/2026/02/02/report-ashes-of-creation-developer-intrepid-studios-shuts-down-weeks-after-the-mmos)
- Content treadmill at AAA scale: Amazon said continuing New World content was "no longer sustainable" after 4 years. — [MMORPG.com](https://www.mmorpg.com/news/new-world-aeternum-ending-development-with-season-10-after-amazon-layoffs-servers-remain-through-2026-2000136447)
- Team-size strain: Pax Dei's studio said building an MMO with 60 people was already a big challenge, and that progress would slow after it cut to 43. — [Massively OP](https://massivelyop.com/2025/05/06/pax-dei-studio-lays-off-nearly-30-of-its-team-but-says-the-mmorpg-is-still-on-track/)
- Retention and design friction: T&L's decline was attributed to progression and monetization systems, PvP, and guild-oriented design. Brighter Shores' decline was attributed to shallow combat and unrewarding progression. — [MMOBomb T&L](https://www.mmobomb.com/how-heck-did-throne-liberty-lose-95-of-players); [MMOBomb Brighter Shores](https://www.mmobomb.com/from-launch-to-life-support-brighter-shores-lost-97-of-players)
- **[LOW-QUALITY SOURCE: forum anecdotes]** Server hosting: a 12-core, 48-64GB dedicated server is about $500-600/month. Tiny indie projects report single-digit dollars per month. City of Heroes private servers reported about $4,700/month. — [MMORPG.com forums](https://forums.mmorpg.com/discussion/480937/how-much-does-it-cost-to-run-an-mmorpg); [WebHostingTalk](https://www.webhostingtalk.com/showthread.php?t=1185451)

### Inferences
- At indie CCU levels (hundreds to low thousands), hosting runs roughly hundreds to low thousands of dollars per month, which is manageable. The real fixed cost is engineering time for live-ops, GM work, anti-cheat and anti-bot, and exploit fixes. With 2-5 engineers, that time comes directly out of content production.
- Pre-selling an MMO through Kickstarter, founder packs, or land sales creates legal and reputational liability when scope outruns the team. That is the Chronicles of Elyria outcome, and Ashes shows it at larger scale.
- A launch-spike-then-decline curve is the default. A small MMO should plan around the post-decline population (for example 5-10% of peak) and make sure the world still feels alive at that size (fewer servers, megaserver or instance design, NPC or bot fill).

### Gaps
- No verified GDC talk or developer postmortem with a line-item indie MMO operating budget was found in this pass.
- Star Citizen crowdfunding totals were not verified in this pass.

## 6. Scoped alternatives that capture the appeal of MOBAs and MMOs

### Takeaway
The evidence strongly favors scoped-down formats that keep the fantasy (persistent progression, a shared world, competitive depth) while removing the need for large live populations and constant content:
- **Co-op survival with self-hosted or dedicated servers**: Valheim sold 12M+ copies with a 5-person core team.
- **PvE or single-player "extraction"**: Escape from Duckov sold 3M copies in about 3 weeks.
- **Idle and incremental "MMOs"**: IdleOn (solo), Melvor Idle.
- **Auto-battlers with async PvP**: Super Auto Pets (2 devs).

What these share is that the game stays fun with 1-10 players, or with no one online at the same time.

### Cited Findings
- **Valheim** (Iron Gate): developed by a five-person team. It sold 5M+ copies in its first month, 10M+ by April 2022, and 12M+ by September 2023. The studio later grew modestly and has said it deliberately avoids large expansion. Wikipedia reports a full 1.0 release on 2026-09-09 alongside PS5 and Switch 2 versions. — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/valheim-has-sold-over-10-million-copies/); [GameDev Reports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/valheim-sales-have-exceeded-12m-copies); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valheim); [ixbt.games](https://ixbt.games/en/news/2025/10/07/sozdateli-valheim-obieiasnili-pocemu-ne-rassiriaiut-komandu-daze-posle-millionov-prodannyx-kopii.html)
- **Escape from Duckov** (Team Soda, a PvE extraction shooter parody of Tarkov with no required PvP population): launched on 2025-10-16. It sold 1M copies by Oct 22, 2M by Oct 28, and 3M by Nov 8, 2025, with a Steam CCU peak above 300k. — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/escape-from-duckov-has-sold-2-million-copies-in-two-weeks); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Escape_from_Duckov); [Niko Partners](https://substack.nikopartners.com/p/escape-from-duckov-sells-over-2-million)
- **Legends of IdleOn** (solo dev, idle MMO): see section 4. The strongest solo-dev "MMO" commercial case, though revenue figures are estimates.
- **Melvor Idle** (a RuneScape-inspired idle game, originally a solo dev): **[EST]** 500k-1M Steam owners, with estimated lifetime gross of about $6.7M and net of about $2.0M. — [SteamSpy](https://steamspy.com/app/1267910); [Steam Revenue Calculator](https://steam-revenue-calculator.com/app/1267910/melvor-idle)
- **Super Auto Pets** (Team Wood Games, a two-person team; asynchronous auto-battler, so no real-time matchmaking liquidity needed): released on Steam in Sept 2021 and on mobile in Feb 2022, with 1M+ Google Play downloads. **[EST, dated Jul 2022]** Sensor Tower estimated about $80k iOS revenue at that time. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Auto_Pets); [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.teamwood.spacewood.unity&hl=en_US); [Medium/CodeX](https://medium.com/codex/why-super-auto-pets-is-so-good-and-what-is-still-to-come-5c02854c675b)
- **Deadlock** shows that hero-shooter and MOBA hybrids are in demand, but only at Valve scale. **Supervive** and **Omega Strikers** show that "MOBA-lite" live PvP still dies without liquidity (see section 1).
- The OSRS record (about 240k CCU in 2025) and Tibia's profitability show strong, lasting demand for low-fidelity, skilling-focused, old-school persistent worlds. This is the fantasy that IdleOn and Melvor Idle deliver in idle form. — [TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/old-schoolrunescape-concurrent-player-count-high-record-240000-august-2025/); [MMOBomb](https://www.mmobomb.com/news/tibia-oldest-active-mmo-reported-profits-of-14-5-million-euros-last-year)

### Inferences
- **Recommended direction for a 2-5 engineer team (inference):**
  1. Co-op survival or crafting with self-hosted or dedicated servers (Valheim model). Players host, 1-10 players per world, premium price, and no liquidity problem.
  2. PvE or asynchronous extraction and roguelite progression (Duckov model).
  3. Idle or incremental games with MMO-style social features: leaderboards, trading, guilds, async co-op (IdleOn, Melvor). Server load is low and they work well on mobile and Steam.
  4. Asynchronous PvP such as auto-battlers or ghost-data matchmaking (Super Auto Pets). Matches can be served from stored snapshots of other players' teams, which removes the need for concurrency.
  5. If real-time PvP is essential: 1v1 or 2v2 with bots, cross-play and single-region-first launch, and a premium price rather than F2P, so revenue doesn't depend on a large whale population.
  6. If a persistent world is essential: a small-shard "MMO-lite" with instanced or megaserver design, launched with a realistic cap and a low-fi art style (OSRS and Tibia prove the art style is not a barrier). Budget for a 90%+ post-launch CCU decline, and avoid crowdfunding pre-sales for undelivered features.
- The common thread among small-team successes is that they monetize a premium purchase or light IAP from an audience that gets full value playing alone or with friends. The common thread among failures is that they needed thousands of strangers online at once.

### Gaps
- Enshrouded (Keen Games) sales and team-size data were not gathered. Keen is also much larger than 5 people.
- No sourced data on **Bloons TD Battles** revenue, or on Legends of IdleOn's team growth after launch (whether LavaFlame2 has since hired).
- Extraction-shooter failure rate (for example small multiplayer extraction games that died from low population) was not quantified. Duckov's success is PvE-only, and that distinction matters.
- No sourced evidence was found for text-based or browser MMOs (MUD-style) as commercial ventures in 2020-2026.
