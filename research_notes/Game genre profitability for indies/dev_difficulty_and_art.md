# Development Difficulty, Cost, Timeline, and Art Dependency by Genre (for a 2-5 person engineer-heavy indie team)

Research date: 2026-09-29. Budget: ~28 tool calls. Most figures are 2024-2026. Older data is flagged. Where only aggregator or competitor sources were available, this is noted.

---

## Q1. What team sizes, dev times, and budgets do successful indie examples have, by genre?

### Takeaway
Most of the breakout indie hits in the requested genres were built by 1-7 people over 1-4 years, often with very little cash (Vampire Survivors spent about £1,100 on assets; Peak cost less than $200k). The single-player, systems-heavy genres (survivors-like, deckbuilder, automation, colony sim) are dominated by solo or duo engineer-designers who either made their own simple art or bought asset packs. Co-op "friendslop" hits (Lethal Company, R.E.P.O., Peak, Schedule I) show that small teams can ship multiplayer if they use relay/P2P services instead of authoritative dedicated servers. MMOs and live service games (Albion Online, Deep Rock Galactic) needed teams of 60-120+ to sustain.

### Cited Findings

**Roguelike / roguelite / deckbuilder**
- Balatro (poker roguelike deckbuilder): solo developer LocalThunk. First prototype ("CardGame") in December 2021, built alongside a full-time IT job. He went full-time in January 2023, commissioned the soundtrack from Luis Clemente in March 2023, and released in February 2024. That is about 2 years 2 months from prototype to launch. He reports serious stress-related health issues in the six months before launch. — [LocalThunk, "The Balatro Timeline"](https://localthunk.com/blog/balatro-timeline-3aarh); [Insider Gaming](https://insider-gaming.com/localthunk-the-balatro-timeline-solo-dev/)
- Balatro's budget was not disclosed (side project). Team of 1, about 2.5 years. — [SteamPageAnalyzer budget compilation (aggregator, cites Playstack)](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/famous-indie-game-development-budgets)
- Slay the Spire: 2 developers plus contracted audio, about 3.5 years, self-funded. The budget is inferred, not documented. (Older data: released 2019.) — [SteamPageAnalyzer (aggregator)](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/famous-indie-game-development-budgets)

**Survivors-like / bullet heaven**
- Vampire Survivors: solo developer Luca Galante started in 2020 while unemployed. About 1 year to Early Access at $3. He spent about £1,100 on assets, art, and music, including pre-purchased asset packs with Castlevania-style sprites. After the game's success he brought on more developers to improve the engine and expand the game. poncle reports 27 million players (2026). — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vampire_Survivors); [SteamPageAnalyzer (cites PC Gamer interview)](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/famous-indie-game-development-budgets)
- Brotato: solo developer Thomas Gervraud (studio Blobfish, Lyon). It was his 4th game, made in Godot. He has made small games full-time since 2018. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brotato); [Blobfish About](https://www.blobfish.dev/about/); [Godot showcase](https://godotengine.org/showcase/brotato/)

**Automation / factory**
- Factorio: Wube started as "a garage company" of 2 programmers and 1 artist and grew to 31 in-house staff and contributors. The game has been in development for about 13 years, and Wube has announced its next major update will be its last. — [Factorio Team page](https://www.factorio.com/game/about); [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/sim/factorios-next-major-update-will-also-be-its-last-as-its-developer-announces-it-has-reached-a-good-place-to-conclude-active-gameplay-development/)
- Mindustry (automation plus tower defense): sole developer Anuken, who also makes all the art and music. Open source on GitHub. — [Mindustry wiki (fandom)](https://mindustry-unofficial.fandom.com/wiki/Anuken); [GitHub Anuken/Mindustry](https://github.com/Anuken/Mindustry)
- Shapez (abstract-shape factory game): started by Tobias Springer (tobspr). The original shapez.io is open source. tobspr Games is now a remote studio of full-time staff plus freelancers making Shapez 2 and the upcoming "puzzlez" (2027). — [tobspr.io](https://tobspr.io/); [GitHub shapez.io](https://github.com/tobspr-games/shapez.io); [Saving Content](https://www.savingcontent.com/2026/09/07/the-next-game-from-tobspr-games-is-puzzlez-a-shapez-game-coming-to-steam-in-2027/)
- Dome Keeper (mining roguelite / tower defense): Bippinbits is a two-person studio in Dresden (René and Anne) with occasional outside help, including pixel artist penusbmic. Published by Raw Fury. Multiplayer was added in 2026. — [Dome Keeper Wiki](https://domekeeper.wiki.gg/wiki/Bippinbits); [Xbox Wire](https://news.xbox.com/en-us/2026/04/13/dome-keeper-multiplayer/)

**Colony / management sim, life sim**
- RimWorld: Tynan Sylvester built the first version alone in 2013 with no outside funding. Later credits list 3 programmers plus artists and a composer. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RimWorld); [Tynan Sylvester](https://tynansylvester.com/)
- Dwarf Fortress: 2 developers (the Adams brothers) over 20+ years, funded by about $15K/month in donations before the Steam release. — [SteamPageAnalyzer (cites Heise.de)](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/famous-indie-game-development-budgets)
- Stardew Valley: 1 developer, about 4.5 years, no cash outlay. The estimated opportunity cost was about $200-300K. (Older data: released 2016.) — [SteamPageAnalyzer (developer statement)](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/famous-indie-game-development-budgets)
- Schedule I (open-world crime / business sim, co-op): solo developer Tyler (TVGS, Sydney). About 3 years of development in Unity, with models and animations made in Blender. Early Access began March 24, 2025. A secondary source reports about $151M Steam revenue in 2025 and a 414K CCU peak in the first weekend. TVGS has since opened an office and expects about 4 people by year end. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schedule_I_(video_game)); [fryosstudios](https://fryosstudios.com/how-tyler-created-schedule-i-the-open-world-crime-game-by-a-solo-developer-and-how-small-studios-can-make-a-huge-impact/); [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/life-sim/schedule-1-developer-tvgs-is-an-actual-game-studio-now-with-an-office-desks-and-a-new-guy-named-rob-by-the-end-of-the-year-there-will-likely-be-4-people-working-on-schedule-1/). The revenue figures come from search snippets of secondary sources and are not verified against primary data.

**Co-op multiplayer party / "friendslop" / horror co-op**
- Peak: 7 developers (3 from Aggro Crab, 4 from Landfall). Core production took about 4 weeks as a game jam in Seoul (February 2025), followed by polish. It was announced and launched within about a week. The budget was under $200,000, "mainly just a couple months salaries and the Airbnb, flights, and food for Korea". Priced under $5. It sold 100K in 24 hours and 2M afterward; a later report says 11M copies. Their motto was "the game jam IS the game." — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/production/how-co-op-climbing-hit-peak-achieved-2-million-sales-for-less-than-200-000-); [GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/co-op/after-a-few-months-of-work-led-to-11-million-copies-sold-on-steam-peak-devs-embrace-what-many-companies-refuse-to-learn-were-not-going-to-continually-have-a-graph-go-up/)
- R.E.P.O.: Semiwork (Sweden), reported at about 6-10 developers (a fan-wiki estimate). Made in Unity with Photon Unity Networking (PUN), chosen on Landfall's recommendation. It started as a single-player prototype. Photon reports it scaled "from 0 to hundreds of thousands of concurrent users" without interruption and sold about 8.7M copies in March 2025. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R.E.P.O.); [Photon blog (vendor)](https://blog.photonengine.com/r-e-p-o-multiplayer-success-powered-by-photon/); [Repo Wiki (fan)](https://repogame.fandom.com/wiki/Semiwork)
- Lethal Company: solo developer Zeekerss, who had about 10 years of experience (Roblox, then small Unity horror games). Planned for 6 months of development, but took longer, including a late-2022 crisis where major content was scrapped. Early Access released October 23, 2023. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lethal_Company); [Push To Talk](https://www.pushtotalk.gg/p/how-lethal-company-sold-10-million-copies)

**Survival-crafting**
- Valheim: made by 5 people at launch (Unity). Iron Gate has since grown to 16 people, with only 8 working on Valheim. — [Automaton West](https://automaton-media.com/en/interviews/valheim-developers-continue-to-work-with-only-8-team-members-we-ask-them-why-they-stick-to-small-scale-development-amid-growing-competition/); [GamingBolt](https://gamingbolt.com/valheim-was-made-by-a-team-of-just-5-people-developer-is-in-the-process-of-expanding)

**Co-op shooter (extraction-adjacent)**
- Deep Rock Galactic: Ghost Ship Games was founded in 2016 by 6 veteran developers (from The Division, Far Cry 3, and others) and is now 60 people. Built in UE4 with procedurally generated, fully destructible caves for 1-4 players. — [Ghost Ship Games](https://ghostship.dk/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_Rock_Galactic)

**MMO**
- Albion Online: Sandbox Interactive started in 2012 as a small independent team. The game launched in 2017 and now has 100-120+ staff. Stillfront acquired the studio for €130M in December 2020. It is one of the longest-running Unity MMOs (PC, mobile, Xbox). — [Unity blog](https://unity.com/blog/albion-online-cross-platform-pvp-mmo-architecture); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albion_Online); [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/how-albion-online-devs-built-a-sandbox-mmorpg-across-an-entire-decade)

**Other reference points (older data, pre-2020)**
- Undertale: 1 developer plus guest artists, about 32 months, $51,124 Kickstarter. Shovel Knight: about 5 people, about 15 months, $311,502 Kickstarter. Hollow Knight: 3 people plus contractors, about 2.5 years, A$57,138 Kickstarter plus savings. — [SteamPageAnalyzer (Kickstarter pages)](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/famous-indie-game-development-budgets)

### Inferences
- **Solo developers who are engineers first are the norm for systems-driven hits.** Vampire Survivors, Balatro, Brotato, Mindustry, RimWorld v1, Stardew, Lethal Company, and Schedule I were each built by one person. A 2-5 engineer team is already larger than most of these teams were at launch. The binding constraints are design taste and scope discipline, not headcount.
- **Typical time to first release for hits in favorable genres:** 1 year (Vampire Survivors), 1 month plus polish (Peak), about 2-3 years (Balatro, Schedule I, Lethal Company), about 3.5-4.5 years (Slay the Spire, Stardew). Factorio (13 years) and Dwarf Fortress (20+ years) are long-tail live-development outliers.
- **Cash budgets for 2-5 salaried engineers are mostly salaries.** Peak's under-$200k budget for 7 people over a couple of months is a good benchmark for jam-scale co-op. A 2-5 person team working 2 years at modest salaries would plausibly cost $300k-$1M+. This is my estimate, not a sourced figure.
- **MMOs, MOBAs, and extraction shooters fall outside the viable range for this team.** Albion needed about 5 years to launch and grew to 100+ staff. No comparable 2-5 person MOBA or MMO success was found.

### Gaps
- No reliable, specific data was found for indie fighting games, MOBAs, extraction shooters, puzzle games, tactics games, or city builders by 2-5 person teams within the call budget. Known candidates to research: Into the Breach (Subset Games, 2 core devs), Islanders (3 devs), Baba Is You (solo), Rivals of Aether, and Dark and Darker (not small).
- Semiwork's team size is a fan-wiki estimate. The Schedule I revenue figure is from secondary sources.
- The Deep Rock Galactic team size at launch (vs. 60 now) and budget were not found.
- The claimed Old School RuneScape-like private or indie MMO team sizes were not researched.

## Q2. What multiplayer, netcode, and backend challenges exist, and what do they cost?

### Takeaway
Co-op games for 2-8 players can use relay or P2P hosting (Photon, Steam networking) for roughly $0-$0.50 per CCU per month. Free platform services (Epic Online Services, which includes Easy Anti-Cheat and matchmaking) remove most backend cost. Competitive PvP (extraction shooters, MOBAs, fighting games) needs authoritative servers or rollback netcode, anti-cheat, and matchmaking liquidity. That work is engineering-heavy and carries ongoing costs. Dedicated-server hosting is priced per vCPU-hour plus egress, and the vendor market is unstable: Hathora shut down in May 2026.

### Cited Findings

**Relay / room-based services (co-op)**
- Photon Fusion pricing: free for 100 CCU. The 200 CCU "Plus" bundle costs $95/year. 500 CCU costs $125/month and 2,000 CCU costs $500/month. Premium Cloud above 2,000 CCU is linear at $0.50/CCU. Bandwidth of about 3 GB per CCU is bundled. — [Photon Fusion pricing](https://www.photonengine.com/fusion/pricing); [Photon pricing docs](https://doc.photonengine.com/photon/current/pricing); [Supercraft summary (third party)](https://crux.supercraft.host/blog/photon-fusion-pricing-2026/)
- R.E.P.O. used Photon PUN and scaled to hundreds of thousands of CCU with no outages. The developers said "Most things with Photon have been very quick to get set up." — [Photon blog (vendor case study)](https://blog.photonengine.com/r-e-p-o-multiplayer-success-powered-by-photon/)

**Free backend services**
- Epic Online Services is free on any engine or store. It includes auth/Connect, lobbies/sessions/matchmaking, voice, achievements, leaderboards, player/title storage, and the free tier of Easy Anti-Cheat. Paid EAC tiers (Core, Fortified, Premier) add deeper detection and support. — [Epic announcement](https://www.epicgames.com/site/en-US/news/epic-online-services-launches-free-in-game-voice-and-easy-anti-cheat); [easy.ac](https://www.easy.ac/); [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/epic-has-made-easy-anti-cheat-free-for-game-developers/)

**Dedicated / authoritative server hosting**
- Edgegap bills about $0.069 per vCPU-hour, about $0.0051 per GB-hour of RAM, and $0.10/GB egress. At scale, bandwidth is commonly 30-50% of the bill. — [Gameye comparison (competitor source; treat as potentially biased)](https://gameye.com/cost-comparison/); [Edgegap pricing](https://edgegap.com/resources/pricing)
- Worked example (from a competitor): a 1 vCPU MOBA with 1,000 peak monthly CCU across 6 regions costs about $4,614/month on GameLift vs. about $1,968/month on Edgegap. AWS reportedly made egress free on GameLift gen-6+ instances in June 2026. — [Gameye cost comparison](https://gameye.com/cost-comparison/). This source is a competitor to both and is not independently verified.
- AWS published a guide on hosting an Unreal game "for under $1 per player" on GameLift. — [AWS for Games blog](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/gametech/how-to-host-your-unreal-engine-game-for-under-1-per-player-with-amazon-gamelift/)
- Vendor risk: Hathora announced on March 4, 2026 that it would shut down its game-server hosting on May 5, 2026 after being acquired by Fireworks AI (an AI inference company). Stormgate had to rush out an offline mode, and Nitrado GameFabric was named as the migration path. — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/stormgate-rushing-offline-mode-after-losing-server-access-to-an-ai-company); [PCWorld](https://www.pcworld.com/article/3105695/ai-is-coming-for-your-online-gaming-servers-next.html); [Gameye blog](https://gameye.com/blog/game-server-shake-up-2026/)

**Rollback netcode (fighting games, precise PvP)**
- The hard part of rollback is making the game rollback-compatible, not the library itself. That means fast state save/load, game logic cheap enough to re-simulate about 7 frames within one frame, and debugging desyncs. Releasing the GGPO source did not make rollback easy. — [yellowafterlife, "A story about netcode"](https://yellowafterlife.substack.com/p/a-story-about-netcode); [SnapNet, Netcode Architectures Part 2: Rollback](https://www.snapnet.dev/blog/netcode-architectures-part-2-rollback/)
- Rollback requires the whole simulation state to be serializable. Restoring particle and audio state is especially hard. Indie examples using GGPO include Skullgirls, Divekick, and Lethal League. — [SnapNet](https://www.snapnet.dev/blog/netcode-architectures-part-2-rollback/); [Wikipedia: Netcode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netcode)

**MMO scale**
- Albion Online's architecture writeup describes building a PvP MMO that scales across platforms with a small team. They chose a strong technical foundation early to support large CCU. — [Unity blog](https://unity.com/blog/albion-online-cross-platform-pvp-mmo-architecture)

### Inferences
- **Cost tiers by multiplayer model** (derived from the pricing above):
  - Single-player: $0.
  - P2P or Steam-relay co-op: about $0.
  - Photon-style relay co-op: $0 up to 100 CCU, then about $0.25-0.50/CCU/month.
  - Dedicated authoritative servers: roughly $2-5 per peak CCU per month for session-based games, based on the MOBA example ($1,968-4,614 for 1,000 CCU). Engineering and ops staff cost more than hosting.
  - Persistent MMO: hosting plus a 24/7 operations burden, with no reliable small-team figures.
- **Why co-op hits keep coming from tiny teams:** co-op tolerates host-authoritative or relay networking, cheating matters little, and no matchmaking liquidity is needed because friends invite each other. Competitive genres need all three.
- **Engineers are well suited to netcode.** Deterministic simulation, rollback, and server orchestration play to their strengths, but live-ops and anti-cheat create ongoing burden rather than a one-time cost. Relying on a single hosting vendor is now a demonstrated risk (Hathora).

### Gaps
- No primary, current Steam Networking (Steamworks relay) pricing page was fetched. It is generally understood to be free for Steam games, but this was not verified here.
- Unity Gaming Services (Multiplay/Relay/Lobby) pricing after 2025 was not collected.
- No sourced per-CCU hosting cost for a real indie extraction shooter was found.
- No authoritative data was found on anti-cheat effectiveness or the cost of cheating for small PvP indies.

## Q3. How content-hungry is each genre (hours of play per unit of art)?

### Takeaway
Systemic and procedural genres (roguelike, deckbuilder, survivors-like, automation, colony sim, incremental) turn small art sets into very long play times because replay comes from system combinations, not bespoke assets. Handcrafted linear, narrative, and MMO content scales badly: every hour of play needs new art, levels, and writing. No quantitative "hours per asset" dataset exists, so this section relies on case evidence.

### Cited Findings
- Vampire Survivors reached Early Access with about £1,100 of assets, art, and music, largely pre-made asset packs, and still became a mass hit. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vampire_Survivors); [SteamPageAnalyzer](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/famous-indie-game-development-budgets)
- Deep Rock Galactic's replayability rests on procedurally generated, fully destructible caves for 1-4 players, not handcrafted levels. — [Deep Rock Galactic on Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/548430/Deep_Rock_Galactic/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_Rock_Galactic)
- Shapez uses purely abstract geometric shapes as its core content, and its developer continues to build a franchise on this minimal visual language (Shapez 2, puzzlez). — [tobspr.io](https://tobspr.io/); [GitHub shapez.io](https://github.com/tobspr-games/shapez.io)
- Mindustry was made by one person who also produced all the art and music. — [Mindustry wiki](https://mindustry-unofficial.fandom.com/wiki/Anuken)
- Dwarf Fortress sustained 20+ years of development and play with 2 developers, famously using ASCII/tile graphics before the Steam version. — [SteamPageAnalyzer](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/famous-indie-game-development-budgets)
- Peak shipped a complete co-op game at under $5 from about a month of jam production. Its content depth was judged adequate for the price. — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/production/how-co-op-climbing-hit-peak-achieved-2-million-sales-for-less-than-200-000-)
- Factorio's upcoming expansion (Space Age, released 2024) was billed as about as big as the base game. It kept adding content with a team that grew to 31. — [NME](https://www.nme.com/news/gaming-news/factorio-expansion-will-be-as-big-as-the-base-game-3154737); [Factorio Team](https://www.factorio.com/game/about)

### Inferences
Qualitative content-hunger ranking (low = few assets for many hours):
- **Very low:** incremental/idle (mostly UI and numbers), Dwarf Fortress-style colony sims, abstract automation (Shapez), puzzle games with a rule-based mechanic (though puzzles need hand-designed levels, which costs design time rather than art).
- **Low:** survivors-like (a handful of enemy sprites, weapons, VFX), deckbuilders (card art is the main cost: roughly 100-300 cards each need an illustration or icon), roguelikes with procedural maps.
- **Medium:** automation with a fuller art style (Factorio-scale sprite work), colony and city builders (many building, character, and animation variants), tactics games (units, maps, portraits), co-op party games (short sessions, but each map or mode needs bespoke art).
- **High:** survival-crafting (3D biomes, creatures, crafting items; Valheim needed 5 people), co-op horror and extraction (3D environments, monsters, animation; R.E.P.O. and Lethal Company offset this with lo-fi 3D and proc-gen facility layouts), fighting games (each character needs hundreds of animation frames or 3D animations, plus balance).
- **Very high:** MOBAs (dozens of heroes, each with a model, animations, VFX, and balancing), MMOs (worlds, gear sets, quests; Albion has 100+ staff).

### Gaps
- No quantitative study of "hours of play per art asset" by genre was found. The ranking above is my own judgment from case evidence.
- Card and animation counts for specific games (e.g., Balatro joker count, fighting game frame counts) were not collected within budget.

## Q4. How well does each genre tolerate cheap art, and what art strategies work for engineer-heavy teams (including Steam's AI rules and player sentiment)?

### Takeaway
Engineer-heavy teams have proven routes to shippable art:
- Buy asset packs, as Vampire Survivors did.
- Use CC0 libraries such as Kenney (30,000+ assets) and low-cost Synty low-poly packs.
- Commission pixel art cheaply ($20-70 per animated character sprite on the freelance market).
- Lean on abstract, minimalist, or procedural visuals.

Generative AI art is legal on Steam but must be disclosed if players see it. The January 2026 update narrowed disclosure to player-facing content. Player sentiment is strongly hostile (85% negative in a late-2025 survey), which is a real reputational risk for indies.

### Cited Findings

**Asset stores and free libraries**
- Kenney has released over 30,000 assets, all CC0 (public domain), covering 2D and 3D. They can be used commercially for free. — [Kenney assets](https://kenney.nl/assets); [Godot Forum](https://forum.godotengine.org/t/kenneys-assets-free-and-creative-commons-cc0/36658)
- SyntyPass (Synty's all-access low-poly subscription): the annual plan applies $120 USD of credit (12 months) at checkout. The monthly plan has a 3-month minimum term. The exact current price was not confirmed. — [SyntyPass](https://syntystore.com/products/syntypass); [Synty FAQ](https://syntystore.com/community/faq)
- Vampire Survivors used pre-purchased asset packs, including Castlevania-like sprites, and spent about £1,100 in total on art, assets, and music in year one. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vampire_Survivors); [SteamPageAnalyzer](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/famous-indie-game-development-budgets)

**Outsourcing and contract costs (pixel art)**
- Freelance pixel artist rates run from about $15/hour (entry level) to about $50/hour (established). Animated character sprites (idle/walk/attack) cost about $20-70+ per character. Tiles cost $1-3 each or $20-60 per tileset. Simple idle sprites can cost as little as about $6.50. — [2D Will Never Die](https://2dwillneverdie.com/blog/how-much-do-sprites-cost/); [Fiverr pixel art](https://block.fiverr.com/hire/pixel-art); [Pixel Frog commission info](https://pixelfrog-assets.itch.io/commission-info). Note: these are low-end marketplace rates. Senior or console-quality artists cost far more, and the Game Developer "Pricing Pixelart" article is an older (pre-2020) reference on why rates vary. — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/pricing-pixelart-or-quot-where-can-i-get-free-pixels-quot-)
- Dome Keeper, a two-person engineering-and-design studio, brought in pixel artist penusbmic as occasional outside help. This is an example of the "contract a pixel artist" model. — [Dome Keeper Wiki](https://domekeeper.wiki.gg/wiki/Bippinbits)
- Balatro contracted its music (Luis Clemente) while the solo developer made the visuals. — [LocalThunk blog](https://localthunk.com/blog/balatro-timeline-3aarh)
- Schedule I's solo developer made his own 3D models and animations in Blender with a simple, stylized low-fi look. — [fryosstudios](https://fryosstudios.com/how-tyler-created-schedule-i-the-open-world-crime-game-by-a-solo-developer-and-how-small-studios-can-make-a-huge-impact/)

**Steam's AI content rules (2024-2026)**
- Valve introduced AI disclosure in January 2024 and rewrote the form on January 16, 2026. The core rule: disclose AI-generated content that ships in the game and that players consume. AI used only in development (coding assistants, bug detection, productivity tools) is exempt. — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/software/ai/steam-updates-ai-disclosure-form-to-specify-that-its-focused-on-ai-generated-content-that-is-consumed-by-players-not-efficiency-tools-used-behind-the-scenes/); [Production Alchemist summary](https://www.productionalchemist.com/p/steam-ai-disclosure-rules-2026-what-indie-devs-need-to-know)
- There are two categories:
  - **Pre-generated:** content "created with the help of AI tools."
  - **Live-generated:** created by AI at runtime. The developer must describe the guardrails that prevent illegal content. Live-generated Adults Only sexual content is banned outright.
  — [Production Alchemist](https://www.productionalchemist.com/p/steam-ai-disclosure-rules-2026-what-indie-devs-need-to-know); [IndieForGames](https://indieforgames.com/steam-ai-disclosure-rules/) (secondary summaries; the primary Steamworks page was not fetched)
- About 8,000 games disclosed AI use in the first half of 2025, up from about 1,000 in all of 2024. Another secondary source reports that about 20% of new Steam games carried AI disclosures in 2026. — [search summary of Tech-Insider / aggregators](https://tech-insider.org/steam-ai-disclosure-2026/) (secondary and not verified against Valve data)

**Player sentiment toward AI art**
- Quantic Foundry surveyed gamers from October to December 2025. 85% reported a negative view of generative AI use in games, 62% "very negative", and only about 7.6% approved. Quantic Foundry called such skew rare in its years of gamer surveys. Negativity was strongest among players motivated by story and design. — [Quantic Foundry (Dec 18, 2025)](https://quanticfoundry.com/2025/12/18/gen-ai/) (page returned 403 on direct fetch; figures from search snippet); [Boing Boing summary](https://boingboing.net/2025/12/20/survey-finds-very-negative-attitude-toward-gen-ai-in-games.html)
- Several indie Steam games faced review-bombing in 2025 after AI-generated assets were discovered. Indie developers themselves are the developer segment least convinced of AI's benefits. — [WebProNews](https://www.webpronews.com/ai-divides-2025-gaming-backlash-innovation-and-ethical-challenges/); [SlashData](https://www.slashdata.co/post/what-game-developers-actually-think-about-ai) (secondary; specific review-bombed titles not identified)

### Inferences
Genre tolerance for minimalist, procedural, pixel, or asset-store art (high = art is not a barrier):
- **High:** incremental/idle, automation (Shapez, Mindustry), colony sim (RimWorld's simple top-down sprites, Dwarf Fortress ASCII), survivors-like (asset packs worked), roguelike and deckbuilder (Balatro's pixel-and-shader look was made by the programmer), puzzle.
- **Medium-high:** co-op party and friendslop (deliberately lo-fi 3D reads as charm in Lethal Company, R.E.P.O., and Schedule I; physics comedy carries it), tactics (readable sprites are enough).
- **Medium:** survival-crafting (Valheim's low-res textures plus strong lighting show that engineering-driven rendering can replace asset fidelity, though creatures and biomes are still needed), city builder (Islanders-style minimalism works, but the genre's audience often expects detail).
- **Low:** fighting games (character art and animation are the product), MOBAs (hero readability and skin-driven monetization), MMOs, and extraction shooters (expected to be competitive with AAA-looking peers).

Practical playbook for an engineer-heavy team:
1. Pick a genre whose fun lies in the simulation or rules.
2. Lock in a constrained visual language early: abstract shapes, 1-bit or low-resolution pixel art, or flat low-poly with a limited palette.
3. Put engineering into shaders, lighting, post-processing, juice/VFX, and procedural generation, which is where engineers can add "art value."
4. Prototype with Kenney CC0, then buy a coherent pack family (Synty or one itch artist's packs) rather than mixing styles.
5. Contract a single pixel or 3D artist for key art: capsule, trailer thumbnails, hero characters. The Steam capsule is the highest-leverage art spend.
6. Avoid visible generative AI art given the 85% negative sentiment and disclosure requirements. AI for code and tooling needs no disclosure.

### Gaps
- Current per-unit prices for 3D modeling contractors and Steam capsule/key-art commissions were not collected.
- The primary Steamworks AI-disclosure documentation was not fetched directly. The rules above rely on consistent secondary summaries.
- Specific review-bombed AI-art indie titles and their sales impact were not documented.
- The current Unity Asset Store licensing and pricing changes were not researched.

## Q5. Which engine suits an engineer-heavy team (Godot, Unity, Unreal, Bevy, custom)?

### Takeaway
Unity still ships the most Steam games (51% of 2024 releases) and powered most of the co-op hits (Lethal Company, R.E.P.O., Schedule I, Valheim, Albion), largely because of its asset store and Photon ecosystem. Godot is rising among newer indies (11% primary use; shipped Brotato), and its open source licence appeals to engineers. Unreal leads mindshare overall but is heavier and suits 3D and shooters (Deep Rock Galactic). Bevy (Rust) is still pre-1.0 and risky for a commercial title. Custom engines work for 2D systems games (Balatro used LÖVE; Factorio and Mindustry used custom or framework-level code).

### Cited Findings
- Steam releases in 2024 by engine: Unity 51%, Unreal 28%, Godot 5%. — [GameDiscoverCo](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/hows-pc-game-engine-usage-changing); [Video Game Insights via GameDevReports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/video-game-insights-game-engines)
- The GDC 2026 State of the Game Industry survey reports Unreal as the primary engine for 42% and Unity for 30%. Unreal dominates AA (59%) and AAA (47%). 54% of developers at older indie studios still use Unity, and 11% of newer indie developers use Godot. — [GDC 2026 State of the Industry](https://gdconf.com/article/gdc-2026-state-of-the-game-industry-reveals-impact-of-layoffs-generative-ai-and-more)
- Unity examples:
  - Lethal Company, R.E.P.O. (with Photon PUN), Schedule I, Valheim, and Albion Online — [Wikipedia: Lethal Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lethal_Company); [Wikipedia: R.E.P.O.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R.E.P.O.); [Wikipedia: Schedule I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schedule_I_(video_game)); [Automaton](https://automaton-media.com/en/interviews/valheim-developers-continue-to-work-with-only-8-team-members-we-ask-them-why-they-stick-to-small-scale-development-amid-growing-competition/); [Unity blog](https://unity.com/blog/albion-online-cross-platform-pvp-mmo-architecture)
- Godot example: Brotato — [Godot showcase](https://godotengine.org/showcase/brotato/)
- Unreal example: Deep Rock Galactic (UE4) — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_Rock_Galactic)
- Bevy 0.18 (March 2026) added an editor preview and a more mature ecosystem, but it is still pre-1.0 with frequent breaking changes. Opinions differ on whether it is ready for commercial games. Tiny Glade, often cited as the flagship, began in Bevy but uses a customized or partly custom engine. Molecoole shipped on Steam with Bevy. — [StraySpark](https://www.strayspark.studio/blog/bevy-rust-game-engine-2026-indie-guide); [EngineRanked](https://engineranked.com/article/bevy-engine-review-2026/); [Wikipedia: Tiny Glade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiny_Glade); [bevy_awesome_prod](https://github.com/Vrixyz/bevy_awesome_prod/)
- Mindustry is open source and developed by one person, with its codebase on GitHub. — [GitHub Anuken/Mindustry](https://github.com/Anuken/Mindustry)

### Inferences
- **Unity** is best for 3D co-op and friendslop, and for teams that want asset-store art (Synty and most packs target Unity first) and turnkey networking (Photon, Netcode for GameObjects).
- **Godot** is best for 2D systems games (survivors-like, deckbuilders, automation, colony sims) when the team values open source, fast iteration, and no licensing risk. Its multiplayer and 3D tooling is less proven at scale; no Godot-based co-op hit comparable to R.E.P.O. was found.
- **Unreal** fits 3D shooters, extraction games, and survival games that need visual fidelity. Its C++ and Blueprint overhead is heavier for a small team, and it pairs naturally with Epic Online Services.
- **Bevy or custom code** suits engineers building simulation-heavy 2D games where the engine is part of the product (Factorio-like scale, deterministic lockstep). The trade-off is building the editor and tools yourself and the risk of API churn.

### Gaps
- I did not verify primary sources for Balatro's engine (LÖVE/Lua) or Factorio's custom C++ engine within budget. These are widely reported but uncited here.
- Unity's runtime-fee reversal and current licensing terms (2024-2026) were not re-verified.
- No benchmark was found comparing Godot 4's multiplayer maturity with Unity's for shipped titles.

## Q6. Comparative scoring matrix: how do the genres score on criteria (a) to (e)?

### Takeaway
For 2-5 strong engineers with little art skill, the best-fit genres are automation/factory, colony/management sims, roguelike/deckbuilders, survivors-likes, incremental games, and small-scope co-op "friendslop" using relay networking. The worst fits are MMOs, MOBAs, fighting games, and extraction shooters, which combine high art demand, heavy servers and live-ops, and matchmaking or anti-cheat burdens that sourced examples show need teams of 60-120+.

### Cited Findings
- The scores below synthesize the cited evidence in Q1-Q5. Key anchors:
  - Solo or duo successes in automation (Mindustry, early Factorio at 2 programmers and 1 artist), colony sims (RimWorld, Dwarf Fortress), survivors-likes (Vampire Survivors, Brotato), and deckbuilders (Balatro) — [Factorio Team](https://www.factorio.com/game/about); [Mindustry wiki](https://mindustry-unofficial.fandom.com/wiki/Anuken); [Wikipedia: RimWorld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RimWorld); [SteamPageAnalyzer](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/famous-indie-game-development-budgets); [Wikipedia: Brotato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brotato); [LocalThunk](https://localthunk.com/blog/balatro-timeline-3aarh)
  - Co-op hits by 1-7 people on relay networking (Peak, R.E.P.O., Lethal Company) — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/production/how-co-op-climbing-hit-peak-achieved-2-million-sales-for-less-than-200-000-); [Photon](https://blog.photonengine.com/r-e-p-o-multiplayer-success-powered-by-photon/)
  - MMOs and live co-op shooters at 60-120+ staff — [Albion (Unity blog)](https://unity.com/blog/albion-online-cross-platform-pvp-mmo-architecture); [Ghost Ship Games](https://ghostship.dk/)
  - Hosting costs of about $2-5 per CCU per month for dedicated servers — [Gameye](https://gameye.com/cost-comparison/)
  - The rollback engineering burden — [SnapNet](https://www.snapnet.dev/blog/netcode-architectures-part-2-rollback/)

### Inferences

| Incremental/idle | 1-2 | 1 | 1 (2 if cloud saves or mobile live-ops) | No example sourced | 5 | Strong, but crowded and low price |
| Survivors-like | 2 | 2 | 1 | Vampire Survivors 1 / ~1 yr; Brotato 1 | 5 (asset packs) | Strong, but saturated |
| Roguelike/roguelite | 3 | 2-3 | 1 | Dome Keeper 2 (plus pixel contractor) | 4-5 | Strong |

- **Sweet spot:** genres where engineering depth is the differentiator (simulation, performance, procedural generation) and the visual language can be abstract. Automation and colony sims score best on combined engineering fit and art tolerance.
- **Co-op friendslop is viable** if the team uses relay or P2P networking, keeps the game at jam scale (Peak's approach), and designs for streamer virality. Server burden stays low because relay costs are cents per CCU and there is no anti-cheat or matchmaking liquidity requirement.
- **Competitive PvP genres** move the burden from one-time development to permanent operations: servers, anti-cheat, matchmaking population, and balance patches. They also have the highest art expectations.

- Market saturation and revenue outcomes per genre are outside this note's scope and presumably covered by other researchers.
