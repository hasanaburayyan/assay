r2ts research · No. 001 · Genre profitability vs. difficulty · September 2026

Build systems,
not servers.

Which genre should a studio of 2–5 strong engineers with no dedicated artist bet on first? We compared Steam revenue data, hit rates, development difficulty and about 30 case studies. The answer is automation and colony sims, where engineering is the moat and simple art is accepted. MOBAs and MMOs are the worst fit on every axis, but the parts you like about them can live on in scoped forms.

Build firstAutomation or colony sim

Premium, $15–20, single-player first. Deckbuilder or roguelite is the runner-up.

Optional side betsJam-scale co-op games

1–4 month "friendslop" prototypes on relay networking. Cheap lottery tickets.

Not as a first gameMOBA or MMO

They fail on player population and live-ops, not on code. Keep the fantasy, change the format.

03 · Revenue by genre

Strategy and sims earn 5–40× more than action

Median revenue by genre varies by more than an order of magnitude, and the ranking has been stable for years. Strategy, sim and survival tags sell more copies and earn 2–3× more per copy than horror, action, puzzle and platformer tags.

05 · The genres you asked about

Every funded MOBA since 2014 has died

No 2–5 person team has shipped a successful traditional MOBA in the period studied. The failures are not about code or art. They are about player liquidity: a team PvP game needs enough strangers online in every region and skill band to fill fair matches fast. When the count drops, queues lengthen, matches get worse, and more players leave. Even 3v3 "MOBA-lite" formats died this way.

The MOBA challenger graveyard

2014 – 2026

Meanwhile the incumbents keep growing. Honor of Kings grossed about $1.6B on mobile in 2025, roughly 10× Mobile Legends.

MMOs: demand is real, small teams still stall

The durable winners are old and low-fidelity, so art is not the barrier. The barrier is the content treadmill, community load and runway. Small-team MMOs follow a spike-then-collapse curve.

Proof of demand
  • Old School RuneScape hit a record ~240.7k concurrent players in Aug 2025.
  • Tibia (1997) made €14.5M profit on €24.5M in 2023, with ~100 staff.
  • Albion Online booked 389 MSEK in 2025, after ~5 years to launch and 100+ staff.
Big launches, 2024–26
  • New World delisted; servers close Jan 2027.
  • Throne and Liberty lost ~95% of its Steam players.
  • Ashes of Creation's studio (123 staff) closed ~7 weeks into early access.
  • Pax Dei cut from ~60 to 43 staff.
Small teams
  • Brighter Shores (RuneScape co-creator): ~20k → ~250 players, −97% in a year.
  • Dreadmyst (solo): 6k+ players, pulled from Steam in ~7 weeks.
  • Project Gorgon (2–3 people): ran out of money in 2023; 1.0 after 8 years, ~$2.2M lifetime (est.).
  • Exception: Legends of IdleOn (solo) dropped real-time combat and the shared world. It is an idle game with MMO social features.
06 · Case studies

Team size doesn't predict the outcome. Format does.

Solo developers sit at both ends of the outcome range, and teams of 40–123 sit at the bottom. The split is whether the game is fun with ten players online, or none, versus needing thousands of strangers at once.

07 · Recommendation

Keep the MOBA/MMO fantasy, change the format

Each thing you like about MOBAs and MMOs has a scoped format that drops the population, live-ops and art burden. If the game finds an audience, leaderboards, trading or small-shard servers can come later, after demand is proven. That is the reverse of the order every failed challenger followed.

A suggested two-track roadmap

months from kickoff · illustrative

The flagship follows the wishlist funnel: Steam page 6–12 months before launch, a demo well before Next Fest, and at least 7–10k wishlists at launch. Demos released months before Next Fest earn about 2.5× the median gain (806 wishlists). Dome Keeper went from ~1k wishlists before its demo to 189k at launch.

08 · Art & tech

Weak art is solvable. Pick the style on purpose.

Many hits shipped cheap or deliberately lo-fi art. The one art spend you can't skip is the Steam capsule image, which drives clicks and creator coverage more than trailers or ads.

Cheap art that works

Vampire Survivors reached early access on about £1,100 of assets, mostly a bought sprite pack. Kenney offers 30,000+ free CC0 assets. Freelance animated pixel sprites run about $20–70 per character. Lethal Company's "retro jank" look was a deliberate texturing trick.

Pay for the capsule

Chris Zukowski advises spending on capsule art before trailers, ads, conventions or PR. In one case a capsule redesign took a YouTuber's video from 74k to 176k views.

AI art is a risk

Steam requires disclosure of player-facing AI content (rules revised Jan 2026). AI used only for code and tools is exempt. In a late-2025 survey, 85% of players viewed generative AI in games negatively, and several 2025 indies were review-bombed over it.

Networking cost

Co-op on relay is nearly free: Photon is free to 100 concurrent players, $125/mo at 500 and $500/mo at 2,000. Epic Online Services gives lobbies, voice and anti-cheat free. Dedicated PvP servers at 1,000 players across 6 regions run about $2,000–4,600/mo before anti-cheat and balance work.

Hosting vendors can vanish

Hathora shut down its game-server business in May 2026 after an AI company bought it. Stormgate had to rush out an offline mode. Player-hosted or relay designs avoid this risk.

Engine

Unity shipped 51% of 2024 Steam releases and most 3D co-op hits (asset store plus Photon). Godot is 5% overall but 11% among newer indies and suits 2D systems games like Brotato. Bevy is still pre-1.0, a poor bet for a first commercial game.
